Paired translation between quasiperiodic physiological waveforms, such as predicting a seismocardiogram from a signal recorded at another body location, has geometric structure: phase is circular, amplitude stays positive, and beat alignment drifts across cycles and subjects. We propose cylindrical geodesic flow matching, which interpolates paired signals along a closed-form geodesic on the phase–amplitude cylinder. On zero-shot PPG and limited-support SCG adaptation benchmarks, it outperforms interpolation baselines and matches or exceeds direct supervised prediction, reducing Hilbert-transform, L2 and DTW distances by up to ~15% over the strongest baseline.
